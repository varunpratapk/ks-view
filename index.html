How to read this table
Signal - the overall verdict on each company
• Early-cycle opportunity: the business is turning up after a weak patch and the evidence is building before it fully shows in reported numbers (e.g. guidance raised, capacity filling up, new capex, demand recovering). This is the main hunting ground.
• Improving: things are getting better, but the evidence is early, partial or from one quarter only - watch for confirmation in the next call.
• Steady compounder: a good business growing consistently, with nothing new changing. Quality is holding, but it is not a fresh inflection - more relevant for reviewing quality holdings than for new early-cycle ideas.
• Stable / no change: little is different from earlier calls.
• Deteriorating: getting worse - guidance cut, demand slowing, margins under pressure or promises missed.
• Avoid: clear, broad deterioration across most areas.
Cycle stage - where the business is in its cycle
• Trough: weak, but may be bottoming. Early recovery: the turn has started (the sweet spot). Expansion: growth well under way. Structural growth: long-term growth not tied to a cycle. Late cycle / peak: margins or growth at highs, risk of normalising. Downturn: declining.
Other columns
• Guidance: did management raise, keep, cut or drop its targets versus earlier calls (▲ raised, ▼ cut).
• Demand / Margins: is the business selling more and earning more per unit than before (▲ better, ▼ worse).
• Capacity: 'Utilisation rising' or 'Adding capacity' usually means demand is filling factories - a classic early-cycle sign.
• Mgmt tone: more confident or more cautious than in earlier calls.
• Delivered on promises: did what management said earlier actually happen - the credibility check.
• Colours: green = good, grey = neutral / no change, amber = mixed or watch, red = bad, white = not discussed.
• ◆ next to the AI score: the formula and Gemini disagree by 2+ points - read the detail before relying on either.
• Amber 'Quarters' / below the grey line: the company's latest call is one quarter old; it moves up once it reports.
Scores
• Formula score (used for ranking): 8-10 = strong, broad evidence of improvement; 6-8 = positive; around 5.5 = nothing has changed; below 4 = deteriorating.
• AI score: Gemini's own overall view of the same calls, for comparison.
How to use the ranking
1. Start at the top: companies with the latest quarter's call and a formula score of about 7 or more.
2. Prefer 'Early-cycle opportunity' or 'Improving' where earlier promises were 'Delivered'; be careful with 'Mixed' or 'Missed' - management may be over-promising.
3. Open the company detail: read 'What changed', the quarter-by-quarter trend, red flags and the key quote.
4. Treat 'Steady compounders' as quality names to monitor, not as fresh turnaround ideas.
5. Use 'Deteriorating' and 'Avoid' to review anything we already own.
6. Companies listed as 'no recent call' are not rated - check why they stopped holding calls.
7. This is a shortlist of what to research next, not a buy or sell list. Valuation is not considered.
How the ratings are made
Source: the latest 4 earnings-call transcripts each company filed on BSE (calls older than 6 quarters are ignored; companies need at least 2), plus the investor presentation or shareholders' letter for the latest quarter, used mainly for exact numbers and to check the call against the slides.
Step 1 - reading: Google Gemini (gemini-flash-latest) reads the calls with a fixed checklist and classifies each company into fixed categories: guidance raised/maintained/cut, demand trend, margin trend, capacity use, management tone, whether earlier promises were delivered, cycle stage and red flags, quoting the evidence. After the first full read, each new quarter's call is compared with the saved analysis of earlier calls; every few quarters all calls are re-read in full.
Step 2 - Formula score (used for ranking): each category earns points as listed below; score = 5.5 + 0.45 x total points, capped between 1 and 10. 5.5 means nothing has changed; higher = more early-cycle evidence.
Step 3 - AI score: Gemini's own overall 1-10 judgement of the same calls, shown for comparison. Where the two scores differ by 2 or more the company is marked with a diamond and deserves a closer look.
Companies whose latest call is more than 1 quarter(s) behind the last completed quarter are not rated and are listed separately as 'no recent call'.
Ranking: companies whose latest analysed call is the newest quarter come first; companies not yet updated for that quarter are listed below them. Within each group, by Formula score, ties broken by AI score.
Points per category:
• Guidance: Raised +2, First given +2, Maintained +0, Lowered -2, Withdrawn -2
• Demand: Accelerating +2, Stable +0, Mixed -1, Slowing -2
• Margins: Expanding +1.5, Stable +0, Mixed -0.5, Contracting -1.5
• Capacity: Utilisation rising +1.5, Adding capacity +1.5, Underutilised -1
• Mgmt tone: More confident +1, Unchanged +0, More cautious -1
• Delivered on promises: Delivered +1, Mixed -0.5, Missed -2
• Cycle stage: Early recovery +1, Late cycle / peak -1, Downturn -1.5
• Red flags: -0.5 each, at most -2 in total
Limitations: AI-generated from what management said on the calls - it can misread or miss context, and does not look at reported financials, valuation or share price. A screening aid to decide what to research, not an investment recommendation. Verify against the source transcripts before acting.
